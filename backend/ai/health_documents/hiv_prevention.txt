HIV PREVENTION METHODS IN ZIMBABWE
===================================

This document provides evidence-based information on HIV prevention methods available to young people in Zimbabwe.


PREP (PRE-EXPOSURE PROPHYLAXIS)
===============================

What is PrEP?
PrEP is a daily medication (Truvada) that prevents HIV infection when taken consistently. It is used by people who are HIV-negative but at higher risk of exposure.

Effectiveness:
- Over 99% effective when taken correctly every day
- Effectiveness drops significantly if doses are missed
- Works best in combination with other prevention methods (condoms)

Cost in Zimbabwe:
- Government health facilities: Free or minimal cost
- Private clinics: ZWL 2,500 - 5,000 per month
- NGOs and health organizations often provide subsidized access

How to Use:
1. Get HIV test to confirm negative status
2. Start daily pill (one tablet per day)
3. Continue for 2-3 weeks to build protection
4. Come back for HIV tests (recommended: every 3 months)
5. Continue taking daily to maintain protection
6. Contact healthcare provider if doses are missed

Side Effects:
- Nausea (usually improves after a few weeks)
- Dizziness
- Fatigue
- Headaches
- Most side effects are temporary

Where to Access:
- Parirenyatwa Hospital, Harare
- Mpilo Central Hospital, Bulawayo
- Provincial hospitals throughout Zimbabwe
- Private clinics in major cities
- NGO health centers

Important Considerations:
- PrEP does NOT protect against other STIs (pregnancy, gonorrhea, chlamydia)
- Use condoms for complete protection
- Requires consistent daily adherence
- May take 20 minutes of pill taking to build levels
- Sexual partners do NOT need to be informed


CONDOMS
=======

What are Condoms?
Condoms are physical barriers that prevent semen containing HIV from entering the body during sexual contact. They also prevent pregnancy and other STIs.

Types Available:
- Male condoms (go on the penis): Most common and accessible
- Female condoms: Less common but available in some clinics

Effectiveness:
- 85-95% effective with correct use
- 82% effective with typical use (including inconsistent use)
- Most effective when used with lubricant
- No protection if condom breaks or slips off

Cost in Zimbabwe:
- Government clinics: Free condoms usually available
- Pharmacies: ZWL 50-100 per box of 3-10
- Youth centers: Often free
- Some bars and entertainment venues: Free or minimal cost

How to Use (Male Condom):
1. Check expiration date and packaging integrity
2. Open carefully - don't use teeth or sharp objects
3. Place on erect penis before any genital contact
4. Leave space at the tip to collect semen
5. Roll down completely
6. After ejaculation, hold base and withdraw before losing erection
7. Remove carefully and dispose in trash (not toilet)

How to Use (Female Condom):
1. Relax and find comfortable position
2. Insert inner ring into vagina like a tampon
3. Outer ring stays outside covering labia
4. Guide penis into opening
5. Can insert up to 8 hours before intercourse
6. Check during intercourse that penis hasn't slipped outside
7. Remove by twisting outer ring and pulling gently

Tips for Better Use:
- Use only water-based or silicone lubricant (not oil)
- Check condom for tears or damage after sex
- Use new condom for each act of intercourse
- Store in cool, dry place
- Don't reuse condoms
- Have backup condoms available

Side Effects:
- None (some report minor allergic reactions if latex-sensitive)
- Use non-latex condoms if allergic

Where to Access:
- Government health clinics (usually free)
- Pharmacies throughout Zimbabwe
- Supermarkets in most towns
- Youth centers and schools
- Online orders to home
- Hotels and entertainment venues
- Peer outreach workers


HIV SELF-TESTING (HST / ORAL TEST)
==================================

What is HIV Self-Testing?
HIV self-testing allows you to test yourself for HIV using a simple kit at home. You take a small sample of oral fluid or blood and see results within 15-30 minutes.

Types of Tests:
1. Oral fluid test: Use swab in mouth (easier, less blood)
2. Finger-prick blood test: Small blood sample (slightly more accurate)

Effectiveness:
- 92-99% accurate when used correctly
- False negatives possible if tested too early (before HIV window period)
- Always confirm with blood test at clinic for positive results

Cost in Zimbabwe:
- Free at government clinics
- Pharmacies: ZWL 500-1,500 per kit
- NGO programs: Often free
- Online: ZWL 400-1,200 per kit

How to Use:
1. Wash hands and mouth (with water only)
2. Read instructions carefully
3. Wait 20-30 minutes after eating/drinking
4. Use swab on outer mouth surfaces and gums
5. Place in testing device
6. Wait 15-30 minutes for results
7. C = Control line (always appears)
8. T = Test line (appears if HIV-positive)
   - Two lines = HIV-positive (needs clinic confirmation)
   - One line = HIV-negative
   - No lines = Invalid test, repeat with new kit

Important Notes:
- Does NOT protect against HIV (it's only a test)
- Does NOT replace regular clinic testing
- Needs confirmation if positive
- Confidential and private
- Early detection means better treatment outcomes
- Can test multiple times, no limit

Accuracy Window:
- Most accurate after 4 weeks of potential exposure
- Can detect HIV in 18 days
- If negative but exposed recently, retest after 3 months

Where to Access:
- Government health clinics (free)
- Pharmacies throughout Zimbabwe
- Online retailers
- NGO distribution programs
- Youth centers
- Mobile health clinics


POST-EXPOSURE PROPHYLAXIS (PEP)
===============================

What is PEP?
PEP is emergency medication taken AFTER possible HIV exposure to prevent infection. It is a temporary course (28 days) of antiretroviral drugs.

When to Use:
- Condom breakage during sex
- Non-consensual sexual contact
- Occupational exposure (healthcare workers)
- Within 72 hours of exposure (preferably sooner)

Effectiveness:
- 81% effective if taken within 2 hours
- 71% effective if taken within 24-48 hours
- 51% effective if taken within 48-72 hours
- Must complete full 28-day course

Cost in Zimbabwe:
- Often free at government hospitals and clinics
- Private clinics: ZWL 3,000-8,000
- Emergency room: Available 24/7

How PEP Works:
1. Come to clinic within 72 hours of exposure
2. Get HIV test (to confirm negative status)
3. Start antiretroviral medication
4. Take medication twice daily for 28 days
5. Get follow-up HIV tests at weeks 6 and 12
6. Manage side effects with support

Side Effects:
- Nausea and vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Headache
- Fatigue
- Usually manageable with medication
- Side effects improve after 1-2 weeks

Where to Access:
- Emergency rooms (24/7)
- Public hospitals
- Major health clinics
- Some private clinics
- Call ambulance (999) or go directly to hospital

Important:
- Time is critical - come within 72 hours
- Bring ID (can be anonymous if preferred)
- Tell staff about exposure (they're trained to help)
- Stick to medication schedule for full protection
- Do NOT take PEP as regular prevention


COMBINATION PREVENTION
=====================

Best Approach:
HIV prevention is most effective when combining multiple methods:

1. Condoms + PrEP = Maximum protection for sex
2. Condoms + Regular testing = Early detection
3. Early HIV treatment = Prevents transmission
4. Safer practices + Communication = Relationship safety

Young People's Prevention Priority:
✓ Know your status (regular testing)
✓ Use condoms consistently
✓ Communicate with partners about sexual health
✓ Consider PrEP if higher risk
✓ Access immediate PEP if condom failure
✓ Seek treatment if HIV-positive (U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable)


TESTING RECOMMENDATIONS
=======================

Who Should Get Tested:
- Everyone is recommend annual HIV test
- More frequently if sexually active or multiple partners
- After condom breakage or unprotected exposure
- Before starting relationship
- During pregnancy
- If partner has HIV

When to Test:
- At least once per year
- After any risky exposure
- Before starting new relationship
- When seeking PrEP
- If symptoms of STI appear

Testing is:
- Confidential and private
- Free at government facilities
- Non-judgmental
- Supportive and counseling-based
- Available to anyone regardless of age

KEY MESSAGES
============

1. HIV is preventable - you have options
2. Regular testing is important - know your status
3. Condoms remain highly effective and accessible
4. If exposed, PEP can prevent infection (within 72 hours)
5. PrEP is available for those at higher risk
6. Combination prevention is most effective
7. Early treatment prevents transmission (U=U)
8. Sexual communication and health are normal
9. All services are confidential
10. You are not alone - seek help and support


NOTES FOR HEALTHCARE PROVIDERS
==============================

- Young people may present with questions about prevention
- Non-judgmental, evidence-based counseling is essential
- Discuss all options - patient chooses best method
- Ensure confidentiality to encourage testing and access
- Refer to specialists for PrEP and PEP initiation
- Follow current Zimbabwe MOH guidelines
- Provide written information in local languages
- Connect to support services and follow-up care
